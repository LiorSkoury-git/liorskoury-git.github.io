2026An earth-printed pavilion for multispecies cohabitation

BUSTAN

BUSTAN is an earthen pavilion built around a living olive tree. Its 178 robotically printed bricks have varied shapes that provide shade, promote airflow, and create niches for other species. The project brings computational fabrication together with dryland cultivation practices to explore how a structure can support a living microclimate.

ContributionComputation lead, building computational models and generating geometry

2021A design-fabrication method for complex timber structures

Controlled Anisotropy

This ITECH thesis investigates a design and fabrication method for complex timber structures. Flax-fiber reinforcement is woven into 3D-printed joints that connect separate timber members. The approach aims to place fibers more precisely around critical connections and use material more efficiently.

ContributionMaster's thesis project coauthor with Tzu-Ying Chen and Simon Treml

2022Mobile robotic timber fabrication in Berlin

TIM at the Stallwächterparty

At the Stallwächterparty in Berlin, digitize wood and IntCDC presented the mobile TIM platform for robotic timber construction. The installation paired the robot with Fancy Fence, an exhibition stand made from stacked timber slats. During the event, the team fabricated a timber table to demonstrate the platform in a public setting.

ContributionComputational design and digital fabrication of the pavilion and the on-site demonstration

2017Reusable bamboo and custom printed joints

The Bamboo Pavilion

Architecture students at Bezalel designed and built a bamboo pavilion for the academy courtyard. Custom 3D-printed joints were fitted to individual poles, allowing the irregular material to form a larger structure. The connection system made the pavilion easy to dismantle and its components available for reuse.

ContributionStudio tutor alongside Barak Pelman, Shachar Abelson, and Michalis Georgiou

2022AdvanceAEC Autumn School workshop

Instructive Human–Robot Collaboration

This AdvanceAEC workshop explored how people and robots can share tasks in timber prefabrication. Participants were introduced to head-mounted augmented-reality instructions and a server-based approach to assigning work among actors. They then applied those ideas in a hands-on collaborative fabrication exercise.

ContributionWorkshop lead alongside Felix Amtsberg, Xiliu Yang, and Aimee Sousa Calepso

2024Collective robotic construction at ROB|ARCH 2024

Exploring Centralization

The ROB|ARCH workshop examined how much control and information a group of construction robots should share. Participants worked with agent-based simulation, digital threads, and sensing methods before programming mobile robots. The hands-on exercises connected software decisions with collective behavior in a physical construction setting.

ContributionWorkshop leader with Samuel Leder

2020A painting robot with a twin control scheme

Physical Twin

Physical Twin is a mobile painting robot controlled by a smaller physical copy of its arm. Potentiometers in the controller send joint positions to the robot, while a joystick moves its wheeled base. The student project demonstrates both direct brush control and playback of recorded movements.

ContributionStudent project collaborator with Ekin Sila Sahin and Simon Treml
